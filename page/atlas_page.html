kotodama · referent binding

Binding Atlas

Base language models acquire the referent of you in five ordered stages. Every base of 0.6B parameters or more trained on trillions of tokens reaches the first three. The fifth stage, tracking you after objects change hands, is never reached zero-shot by a public model up to 8B, at any token count measured up to 36T; at the final checkpoint its swap shift is negative in every one of them. Whether more tokens would get a small model there is unknown. From 14B to 1T parameters it is weak and nearly flat in 10 of the 11 bases with a public size: a zero-shot swap shift of +.01 to +.07, against +.27 to +.82 for static you in the seven of them where that is measured. In these models parameter count and depth grow together. kotodama-tsumugi, a 1.4B model run as 72 effective layers (looped depth, boundary-fitted attention residuals), separates them: it reaches stage 3 by 33B tokens (annealed; on the constant-LR trunk by 40B), the token cost of OLMo-2 13B, against about 420B for OLMo-2 1B. Trained to 110B tokens, it has not reached stage 5.

Part I

Where base models fail

Figure 1

The staircase

The stages appear in the same order in every trajectory measured: OLMo-2 1B, 7B and 13B; SmolLM3 3B; Pythia 410M to 6.9B; kotodama-tsumugi. kotodama-tsumugi follows the same order at a fraction of the token cost (Part III). Each stage is read with a speaker swap. Only the speaker's name on the last line changes, and a bound pronoun moves its answer with the speaker. The hatched stage is where most bases stop.

Figure 2

Not reached at or below 8B; weak above 14B

Dynamic you at each model's final checkpoint (OLMo-2 1B: its 4T pre-anneal checkpoint). A positive swap shift means you follows the addressee; a negative one means you resolves to the speaker. Zero-shot (filled) is negative for every model at or below 8B, for token counts up to 36T, and turns positive between 8B and 14B on both Qwen recipes. All of these are standard-shape transformers whose depth grows with their size; depth at a fixed parameter count is a separate, unmeasured axis here. Nor is this evidence that the stage needs more parameters: no model at or below 8B has been trained far enough to rule tokens out, so whether it is token-reachable at small sizes is unknown. Above 14B it changes little: +.022 for Qwen3 14B, +.054 for Llama-3.1 405B, +.045 for Kimi-K2 at about 1T. In-context (3-shot, hollow) turns positive at smaller sizes and reaches +.24 for Qwen3 14B. The kotodama-tsumugi release (diamond; 1.4B at 110B tokens) reads −.021 zero-shot. Its interval reaches −.013, so under the gauge rule (inverted only when the whole 95% interval lies below −.02) it is not inverted. Of the 1–2B public bases whose static you binds, only Llama-3.2-1B (−.022) is not inverted too; the rest are (−.047 to −.089). It does not bind either.

Figure 3

Stage cost by model size

Training tokens until each stage appears, for one data recipe (OLMo-2) at three sizes. Stage 1 costs about the same at 1B, 7B and 13B. Stage 3 takes about ten times fewer tokens at 13B than at 1B. Stage 5 is absent at the last checkpoint of all three (4T to 5T tokens). kotodama-tsumugi, at 1.4B parameters, reaches stage 1 between 3B and 3.75B tokens and stage 3 between 30B and 33B (the 13B model's token cost at a tenth of its size), and has not reached stage 5 by 110B.

Table 1

Stage 5, taken apart

The dynamic-you items, rewritten along one axis at a time (accuracy at 0-shot, self and addressee queries pooled; chance ≈ .29; about ±.024 per cell). Narrated removes deixis from the whole scene. Spoken is the gauge item itself. Spoken, transfer narrated keeps the chat but states the hand-off as a stage direction. Spoken + vocative names the addressee in the query. Three layers show. (1) Possession transfer itself. Every ~1B model is at chance even when the scene is narrated: kotodama-tsumugi through 110B, Pythia-1.4B, OLMo-2 1B at 4T. SmolLM2 1.7B at 11T and Qwen3 1.7B at 36T lift off chance; from 4B it is easy (.57–.80). (2) The transfer as a speech act (“here, take my cup”) is the mid-size wall: models from 4B to 70B that pass the narrated version stay at .29–.41 when it is spoken. Narrating only the hand-off recovers part of that gap, from almost none (Qwen3 4B) to about three quarters (Qwen3 30B-A3B). (3) Naming the addressee does not help: the vocative variant is at or below the spoken one for every public model, and within about .01 of it for kotodama-tsumugi.

Part III

言霊 紬 kotodama-tsumugi

言霊 kotodama, word-spirit: language as a thing with a life of its own. 紬 tsumugi, spun thread: words woven into more than the sum of their parts. Every neuron of this model, and the circuits behind these probes: Threads.

A 1.42B-parameter model: 52 unique layers at width 1536, with a 20-layer block in the middle run twice for 72 effective layers, and attention-residual boundaries fitted to the network's own layer geometry. The trunk trains at a constant learning rate to 100B tokens; annealed reads are cooldown branches forked from its checkpoints. The release is the 110B branch from the 100B fork. A second anneal from the same fork, with the same training loss, reads .025 bits/byte worse on held-out text, so every single-anneal number here carries that spread. A second initialization seed (seed 7, same data order) was trained to 11B.

The staircase in one run

Swap shift against training tokens for the three stage gauges, seed 17's trunk (filled markers, constant LR to 100B) and its annealed branches (hollow), with seed 7 (small grey markers, to 11B). Static I binds from 3.75B tokens and plateaus at +.19 to +.31 after about 30B. Static you dips below zero (the you = me stage), first binds at +.073 when annealed at 33B and on the trunk itself at 40B (+.077), and reads +.235 at the 110B release. The second anneal from the same fork reads +.142: a single checkpoint's static-you reading carries about ±.05 of anneal-to-anneal spread. Dynamic you (0-shot) inverts from about 4B, deepest at 11B (−.047), and shallows to −.020 at 88B and −.016 / −.021 at 110B, no longer inverted under the gauge rule; it never binds. The markers along the top show where OLMo-2 reaches stage 3 at 13B, 7B and 1B parameters.

Table 3

Battery at the early annealed reads

Accuracy on the binding battery at each annealed branch (chance in brackets where it is fixed). The bookkeeping probes rise steadily from 5B to 33B. The perspective probes hold near their 5B level through 11B and begin to lift at 33B, alongside stage 3. The two seeds differ at 5B (seed 7 forms set binding earlier, seed 17 forms static I earlier) and agree within sampling error at 11B: the seed changes when each circuit forms, not where it settles. Table 4

The release against its trajectory and its class

Stage gauges (swap shifts; above +.02 binds), binding capacity (after arXiv 2609.30634: K50 = how many name–task bindings fit in context before recall falls halfway; the last capacity row is recall of a single binding across 256 tokens of filler) and deixis-v2 accuracy (chance ≈ .33). The two 110B columns are two anneals from the same fork with the same training loss. The spread between them (static you .142 vs .235, K50 3.8 vs 4.8) is the measured anneal-to-anneal variance, so a difference between neighbouring checkpoints on one axis is not a trend. deixis v2 has no SmolLM2 or Qwen3 grid. Capacity tracks recipe and tokens more than parameters on this harness (Pythia is flat from 160M to 6.9B), so no parameter-equivalent size is read from it.

Table 5

Object tracking, with fair examples

Riders v2: boxes go through swaps and moves in narrative or screenplay prose and one box is queried (accuracy; chance .25; about ±.024 per cell). With the same three exemplars for every item (fixed3), accuracy falls well below zero-shot for OLMo-2 1B at 4T and for every kotodama-tsumugi checkpoint (−.05 to −.22): the few-shot penalty seen in training-time probes came from that setup, not from a failure of in-context learning. With exemplars drawn per item (fair1 / fair3 / fair6), few-shot helps every public model here (+.06 to +.11 at fair6) and kotodama-tsumugi through 55B (+.02 to +.08), but not the two 110B anneals (second anneal −.03, release −.045). Zero-shot, the annealed kotodama-tsumugi checkpoints from 33B on (.455–.490) sit above every public checkpoint measured on this harness (best: SmolLM2 1.7B at 8.4T, .420). The public pool is three 1–2B models, so no comparison with larger models is claimed. Object tracking is not stage 5: OLMo-2 1B climbs from .230 at 1B tokens to .385 at 4T while its zero-shot dynamic you never binds.

Appendix

Reading the grid
  • Score = (accuracy − chance) / (1 − chance). 0 is guessing among the candidates, 1 is perfect, below 0 is worse than guessing (a systematic wrong answer — usually the shortcut).
  • Deixis columns come in pairs: the I/you/my version and a 3rd-person twin narrating the same events with names. The twin is the control — same memory load, no perspective. The Perspective gap view is twin minus deictic: how much a model loses purely by having to take a seat.
  • Shortcut-free keeps only items where the answer is neither the last-mentioned person nor the last speaker. Most deixis "success" lives in those two shortcuts; with them removed, nearly everything falls to chance on query-side you — up to 405B.
  • P3 / P2 = three or two people in the scene. P2 you is the structural case: no name tells you who you is; only the turn structure does.
  • Open models are scored by log-probability over the candidates. API models (Claude, OpenRouter rows) can't expose that, so they answer in words: reflex = "reply with only the next word", deliberate = "think it through, then answer". Compare within a scoring type first.

      Methods and instruments
      • Core binding: static lookup (N people each keep an object), code variables (assignments, copies, swaps), 3rd-person updates (objects change hands, partly via pronouns).
      • Deixis v2: chats where ownership is stated only through I/my/you and vocatives; queries ask who has an object (owner), what the speaker holds (self), or what the addressee holds (you). Every deictic item has a name-only twin. Generated procedurally with frozen validity gates; candidates are always the names/objects in play.
      • Battery v3: set binding (several objects per person; exact-set scoring), production deixis (answer the question as the answerer would: me / you / a name; read against a body-hidden null to remove the phrase prior), adjacency (yours resolved only by request→grant structure), aliased Python lists (mutate / rebind / copy traps).
      • Kotodama models: lever arms at 1.42B parameters and 4B tokens (Table 2), and kotodama-tsumugi (Part III): a constant-LR trunk to 100B tokens with cooldown branches (cosine decay to zero over about 10% of the tokens so far, forked from full checkpoints), read with the stage gauges, the battery, binding capacity, the stage-5 decomposition and riders v2.
      • Cells need n ≥ 8 (the human row is the exception — it is a handful of items per cell, shown as small-sample).
