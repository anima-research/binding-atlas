design spec

At each step of the process, we performed ablations to test four levers towards enhancing binding.

Depth was prioritized for serial operations; 52 layers and 1536d wide, vs 16-28 layers and mostly 2048d wide for other open models near this param count. Moonshot AI's Attention ResidualsKimi Team (Moonshot AI), Attention Residuals, arXiv:2603.15031, 2026. (AttnRes) are used to allow each layer to read a learned mix of prior blocks' outputs instead of a typical running sum. Instead of uniform blocks, we pretrained the same model without AttnRes and read layer geometry to identify natural seams and place blocks to maintain inter-layer circuitry. This fit found a 20-layer contiguous block starting from layer 19, which we selected to run twice with the same weights, such that the end result has 72 logical layers (each pass with its own norms).

Our finding is that each piece synergizes with another, accentuating or unlocking benefits. For example, AttnRes (only with fitted boundaries, not uniform) composes with depth to unlock strong early Whether the model keeps track of which items belong to which group, measured at 4B tokens of training.; looped depth provides the same as more layers for free in weights on ICL & binding.

in-context learning

We evaluated base models on how well they pick up patterns (not reliant upon factual recall, much like Raven's matrices) from examples in context, following ICLEval (Chen et al. 2024)Chen, Lin, Zhou, Huang, Jia, Cao, Wen, ICLEval: Evaluating In-Context Learning Ability of Large Language Models, 2024.. An item is scored right when the model ranks the correct answer the highest from a fixed set. Accuracy is then corrected for chance, where 0 is guessing & 1 is perfect, and averaged over nine levels of difficulty per test.

tsumugi's final checkpoint settles at .559, well above the compute trend for open-weight models, Falcon3 3B being the nearest at .536 (pruned and distilled from Falcon3 7B; more below). When the same test uses known facts, such that knowledge offers a shortcut, tsumugi scores higher (.930), as every model does, landing where the compute trend predicts.

Of the 36 fully trained open-weight models we measured, none scores higher until about 150 times tsumugi's training compute; the first, OLMo-2 7B, used 1.8 × 10²³ FLOPs. tsumugi sits 2.4 SD above the trend; with Trend w/ models more than 2 SD away excluded & refitted. Below: RecurrentGemma 9B and Bamba 9B (recurrent hybrids) and Pythia 160M (the smallest model here); above: Falcon3 3B. excluded, 4.5.

referent binding

Referent binding in prose is the task of tracking who is being referred to, and what belongs to them. A simple test of this capability is the following: "John is holding a bat. Jane is holding a flower. They swap items. John is holding the ______", where flower is the answer.

When personal pronouns such as "I/you/me" are used, this task becomes harder; the referent depends on perspective, who the active speaker is, and context clues. This capability is acquired in stages, in trajectories we've analyzed. First, "I" binds cleanly, then "you" when state doesn't shift, and finally "you" while the state changes.

The figure is a test for that case; possession & speakers change, with clear statements of ownership. A model must predict what "you" is holding. We then also swap who speaks the last line, so that "you" becomes the other speaker, dropping the probability of the prior answer.

On this test, tsumugi's average drop in probability is .038, with a 95% CI clearing +.02 = two pp drop on average, with the whole 95% interval above that line. The bar simply shows that the model moves the right way at all, not how well it does so. The value was arbitrary & fixed before any scoring occurred; an improved test derives it from “null twin” chats, where swapping speakers won’t change the answer, and puts the bar where noise alone stops.. tsumugi is the smallest public base model to achieve this; the next is Qwen3 4B. Scale also doesn't guarantee this capability; OLMo-2 7B and 13B don’t pass, nor do GLM-4 32B or Qwen2.5 72B.

world knowledge evals

On nine standard benchmarks (HellaSwag, LAMBADA, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, PIQA, SciQ, WinoGrande, COPA and BoolQ), tsumugi's zero-shot average is 64.1% correct. On Uncheatable EvalTan, Li, Shen, Uncheatable Eval: Dynamic Compression-Based Evaluation of Language Models, 2026., which evaluates compression on unseen human text, tsumugi scores .768 bits per byte, 0.9 SD better than the trend. tsumugi's score here shows that the enhancements in binding & ICL come at little to no cost on knowledge work: it scores ahead of Pythia 1.4B, trained with twice its compute, and within a point of Pythia 2.8B, trained with four times as much, most likely attributable to either more modern architecture or the data mix.

Given the prior results with binding & ICL, and the knowledge-based benchmarks here, tsumugi operates in context like a model much larger; while having world knowledge expected for its compute.

null completions

tsumugi's training data was sourced specifically to avoid as much synthetic or otherwise LLM-generated or altered text as possible. This is explicitly to make it a "true" base with no distillation involved, and therefore a cleaner bar for understanding architectural differences & benchmark trends.

Of the 36 models shown in the ICL chart above, 19 report their pretraining included synthetic or LLM-generated/altered text, and 4 more are distilled from a larger model; 23 in total.

Compute estimates for these models are rough; we don't calculate how much compute went into generating the non-human data nor the compute that went into training the teacher/previous model. An example of this is Falcon3 3B, which was created via pruning the larger Falcon3 7B, then trained on 100B tokens with a distillation objective.
