who is who, in base language models

kotodama-tsumugi on the benchmark

Led by Luxia, with Anima Labs · built with Claude

This page follows one model through the benchmark's tests of who is who. The benchmark's own page explains the tests, the five stages of you and the swap shift, and scores every public model on them.

kotodama-tsumugi is our 1.4B model. Its middle 20 layers run twice with the same weights, so each token passes through 72 layers. It reaches the third stage by 33B tokens of training, in the range where OLMo-2 13B gets there; OLMo-2 1B needs 420–525B. Given three worked examples, it is the smallest base measured that passes the fifth stage. Its swap shift is +.038, with the whole 95% interval above +.02 (the test). The next is Qwen3 4B.

言霊 kotodama, word-spirit: language as a thing with a life of its own. 紬 tsumugi, spun thread: words woven into more than the sum of their parts. A map of every neuron in this model shows the circuits behind these tests.

Its main run trains at a constant learning rate to 100B tokens. To read a checkpoint fairly, a short cooldown is branched off it: a closing stretch of training where the learning rate decays. Those cooled-down reads are the hollow markers below. Two cooldowns were run from the 100B checkpoint with the same data and settings. The release is one, ending at 110B. The other shows how far a cooldown alone moves a reading, and Table 2 compares them. The model card describes the architecture and the training run.

The stages during kotodama-tsumugi's training

Swap shift against training tokens on three stages: I as the speaker (stage 1), you as the person spoken to (stage 3), and you through hand-offs with no examples (stage 5). Filled markers are the main run at a constant learning rate; hollow markers are its cooled-down reads; small grey markers are a second run from another random start, to 11B tokens.

I as the speaker starts to pass at 3.75B tokens and levels off at +.16 to +.25 from about 40B. You as the person spoken to first dips below zero, the stage where the model treats you as me. It first passes at 33B tokens in a cooled-down read (+.065) and at 40B on the main run itself (+.068), and reaches +.192 at the 110B release. The other cooldown from the same checkpoint reads +.127 (Table 2 gives the spread). On the main run, you through hand-offs with no examples sits just below zero from about 4B (−.015 to −.034) and never passes. At 110B the two cooldowns read −.014 and −.012, not reliably backwards. The markers along the top show where OLMo-2 13B, 7B and 1B reach stage 3.

Figure 2

The run against its peers

kotodama-tsumugi's checkpoints against public checkpoints of similar models, all scored the same way, on one of four measures: the average of nine standard benchmarks with no examples (HellaSwag, ARC, PIQA and six more); unseen text, in bits per byte on text published after the training data was collected; and the swap shifts for stages 1 and 3. The x-axis is training tokens or training compute.

The white line is tsumugi's main run; hollow markers are its cooled-down reads, the 110B release among them. Grey lines are public checkpoints of Pythia-1.4B, OLMo-2 1B, OLMo-1 1B and SmolLM2-1.7B. On unseen text, lower is better. On the compute axis tsumugi pays for its repeated layers (1.13e10 FLOPs per token, about 1.3× the 1–2B bases).

Table 1

kotodama-tsumugi's checkpoints on the main tests

Each cooled-down checkpoint of kotodama-tsumugi on the full atlas's main tests, with the atlas's own values (0 is guessing, 1 is always right). Each column names the run (the main run or the second run) and its training tokens. Tests that have worked examples are read with them, the others alone. Each rule-learning row averages the score over versions of the test that get steadily harder.

Lookup rises from .69 at 5B tokens to .98 by 55B, level with the 7–9B bases (.97 to 1.00). The update tests (many refills, variable chains, shared lists) stay at chance through 33B and lift from 55B on (.04 to .22). Static you climbs from .08 at 5B to .49 at the release. Pattern completion reaches .55, second of 12 models at 1–2B parameters. The two seeds differ at 5B (the second run learns lookup and set binding earlier) and agree within .05 on all ten rows at 11B.

Table 2

The release against its trajectory and its class

The release beside its own earlier checkpoints and three public models of its size. The table has three kinds of row. The first four are swap shifts on the stages, and above +.02 passes. The next two test how many name–task pairs ("Ana: plumbing") a model can hold in its prompt, following a published capacity study (Ravulapalli 2026, In-context binding capacity in language models). K50 is how many pairs fit before recall falls halfway to chance. Each trial is written four ways (a list or inline, two question wordings), and K50 is read over all four. The second of the two is recall of one pair across 256 tokens of filler. The last two are accuracies on who-has-what questions (chance ≈ .33).

The two 110B columns are two cooldowns from the same checkpoint, with the same training loss. The spread between them is the measured cooldown-to-cooldown variance. It is .127 vs .192 on you as the person spoken to, and .022 vs .038 on you through hand-offs with examples. K50 reads 4.6 vs 5.1, and held-out text differs by .025 bits/byte. Every single-cooldown number on this page carries that spread, so a difference between neighbouring checkpoints on one axis is not a trend. A second run from another random start (same data order) was trained to 11B. The who-has-what test has no SmolLM2 or Qwen3 reading. Capacity tracks training recipe and tokens more than parameters on this test (Pythia-160M through Pythia-6.9B read about the same), so no parameter-equivalent size is read from it.

On the benchmark's page, kotodama-tsumugi is one row: the release. Its other checkpoints come from two training runs. The main run was cooled down and read at 5B, 11B, 33B, 55B and 88B tokens on its way to the 110B release. A second run, from another random start on the same data order, was read at 5B and 11B.
